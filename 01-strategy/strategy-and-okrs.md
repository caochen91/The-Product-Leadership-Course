# Product Strategy One-Pager & OKRs: Schneider Electric Services Growth Strategy

> Module 1 · Craft an Advanced Product Strategy, ★ Deliverable 1
>
> Your one spine: the **Playing to Win** cascade, one deliberate **hard no**, and an **OKR cascade** that flows directly from it.
> Draft the cascade + hard no in Sprint 1, then add the OKRs in Sprint 2. The goal: specific enough that a skeptical board member couldn't poke a hole in it.

## 0. Chosen scenario

**Path:** _My own initiative: [Scenario 3, Schneider Electric Services Growth Strategy](../scenario/Scenario_3_Schneider_Electric_Services.md)_

I picked it because I'm a Business Development and Product Leader in the Schneider Electric Services division, so this is the real growth decision my team faces.

**Strategic bet:** Strategy B, **Scale Digital Services**, used to make the core (EcoCare and EcoFit) impossible to commoditize. Digital Services is ranked #1 for future growth but only #4 today, which is the largest gap in the portfolio. Strategy A alone loses the price war to independent providers, and Strategy C needs consulting headcount we don't have.

## 1. Playing to Win cascade

| Question | Your choice |
|---|---|
| **Winning aspiration**: winning in the customer's terms, not internal metrics | Be our customers' trusted, go-to service partner for their three biggest challenges: maximizing uptime, preventing unplanned downtime, and improving energy efficiency. We defend the core (EcoCare and EcoFit across our installed base) and, building on it, become known as one of the best digital service providers, combining maintenance, advisory, and AI-driven predictions to optimize all three. |
| **Where to play**: segment, geography, channel, use case (the no's matter too) | **Segments:** customers with critical power and high cost of downtime first: data centers, healthcare, critical industrial sites, and large commercial buildings. **Base:** Schneider's own installed base, starting with sites already under EcoCare. **Offers:** Digital Services attached to EcoCare, with EcoFit triggered by asset-health data. **Channel:** direct for key accounts, partners for the mid-market. **No's:** price-led routine maintenance against independent providers; multi-vendor service at scale; a standalone consulting practice. |
| **How to win**: your differentiator competitors can't easily replicate | **OEM knowledge plus live asset data.** We combine original-equipment expertise, the largest installed base, and remote monitoring to predict failures and prove uptime and energy outcomes. Independent providers can match on price but not on data and OEM depth. Software players have data but no field force or spare parts. We bundle connected EcoCare contracts where the digital layer is included, not optional. |
| **Capabilities required**: what you must be world-class at (build / buy / partner) | **Build:** connected-asset onboarding at scale; predictive analytics on Schneider equipment; remote service centers that resolve issues before a site visit; selling and pricing outcome-based contracts. **Partner:** cloud and AI platforms; OT cybersecurity for connected assets. **Reskill:** field engineers move from scheduled visits to data-driven interventions, so we grow without adding headcount. |
| **Management systems**: the metrics and rituals that reinforce your choices | Monthly services review on connected-asset coverage, digital attach rate, and recurring revenue share (not just bookings). Sales incentives weighted to connected, multi-year contracts. Quarterly pillar review where EcoFit pipeline must cite asset-health data. Field KPIs shift from visits completed to issues resolved remotely and first-time fix rate. |

## 2. Your one hard no

> We will not compete on price for routine, time-based maintenance against independent service providers, because winning that fight means cutting the margin and field capacity we need to fund Digital Services. Customers who only want the cheapest preventive visit are not our target. We will offer them a connected EcoCare contract or let them go.

_How this fits with data centers and AI:_ we go after the data center and AI segments (KR2) through Digital Services, new partnerships, and greenfield projects, not by building a standalone consulting practice that would need headcount we don't have.

## 3. OKR cascade

_One Objective and three Key Results that flow directly from the cascade. Each KR must be a measurable **outcome**, not an output/milestone. The timeframe is three years (Year 1 to Year 3). Values in [brackets] are placeholders for numbers to confirm._

> **Objective:** Become our customers' go-to digital service provider and most trusted partner, making a name for Schneider in the new energy landscape for data centers and AI-related segments, while our core pillars, led by EcoCare, remain the foundation that funds and supports this growth.
>
> - **KR1:** Digital Services revenue, led by digital twin and augmented reality offers, grows from a 10M baseline at an 11% CAGR to **13.68M by Year 3**, making Digital Services one of the top three revenue-generating lines in Schneider Electric Services
> - **KR2:** At least **5M in incremental revenue** from the data center and AI segments of the new energy landscape, from new partnerships or greenfield projects, by Year 3
> - **KR3:** The two core pillars (EcoCare and EcoFit) grow **3% organically plus 5% leveraged growth** from Digital Services and the new markets, and service contract customer satisfaction rises from [today's score] to **[today's score] + 10 points** by Year 3

## 4. AI pressure-test notes

_Run the devil's-advocate prompt (in the Sprint 2 guide). Capture the verdict._ Run on 2026-10-13 against this draft, with Claude as a skeptical Chief Strategy Officer. The "Change or defend?" column holds suggestions for me to accept or reject.

| Prompt question | What the AI surfaced | Change or defend? |
|---|---|---|
| Biggest assumption that could be wrong | That 11% CAGR on a 10M base is enough to make Digital Services a top-three revenue line. EcoCare, EcoFit, and Field Services are ranked above it today and are almost certainly far larger than 10M; if they grow even modestly, 13.68M in Year 3 may still rank fourth. It also assumes customers will pay separately for digital twin and AR, rather than expecting them bundled free into EcoCare. | Suggested: **change.** Get the Year 3 revenue of the #3 pillar and set KR1's target from that gap, or drop "top three" and keep the revenue number. |
| The board question I can't yet answer | "How much are you investing to get 3.68M of new digital revenue and 5M from data centers, where does the money and headcount come from, and what is the payback?" The draft has revenue targets but no investment, margin, or resourcing, while headcount growth is constrained. | Suggested: **change.** Add investment and payback in the financial model (Deliverable 4) and reference it here. |
| KRs that are outputs in disguise | The top-level KRs are revenue and satisfaction outcomes, but: (1) "top three revenue line" in KR1 is a ranking, not a customer outcome; (2) KR3 bundles two metrics, and "5% leveraged growth" can't be measured without a rule for attributing revenue to Digital Services; (3) no KR measures the customer outcomes the aspiration promises (uptime, downtime, energy efficiency). At team level, "offers live with [n] key accounts" is an output. | Suggested: **change.** Define the attribution rule for leveraged growth, and consider a customer-outcome measure (for example, unplanned downtime avoided on connected sites). |
| The "no" I should reconsider | Refusing to compete on price for routine maintenance. Every site lost to an independent provider is a site we stop seeing data from, which shrinks the base Digital Services and EcoFit depend on. A low-cost, remote-first maintenance tier could keep those sites connected at an acceptable margin. | Suggested: **defend, with a tweak.** Keep the no on price-matching, but offer a remote-first entry tier so we keep the asset data. |
| Strategy or wish list? Why? | Closer to a strategy than a wish list because it has a hard no and numeric targets, but it reads partly as a wish list because the three KRs pull in three directions (digital, data centers, core) with no stated shift in money or people between them. | · |

**Harsher round: what a competitor's strategy team would say**

- **Data center specialists:** "5M of incremental data center revenue over three years is a rounding error in a market this hot. Schneider is signaling interest, not commitment, so we keep the greenfield relationships."
- **Independent service providers:** "Schneider has told us they won't fight on price. We'll take the routine contracts and the site access that comes with them, then offer our own monitoring."
- **Digital and automation rivals:** "A 10M digital base growing 11% a year is slow for digital services. If the market grows faster, Schneider hits its KR and still loses share."
- **Overall:** "They're trying to defend the core, scale digital, and win data centers with flat headcount. We only need to beat them on one front."

## 5. Self-diagnostic (6 questions)

- [ ] **Clear**: a new PM could read it and know exactly what we will and won't do
- [ ] **Names the real challenge**: the diagnosis is specific enough to be uncomfortable
- [ ] **Makes a hard bet**: it says no to something valuable
- [ ] **Cascadable**: teams can translate it into their own OKRs
- [ ] **Coherent**: every choice reinforces the others
- [ ] **Committed**: resources are actually moving toward it

**Regional read-through (2026-10-13):** scored from the point of view of a regional business development manager and a regional offer/product manager reading this draft. 1 = not clear, 10 = crystal clear.

| Question | BD mgr | Product mgr | Why, from their side | What would raise it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Clear** | 6 | 7 | "I know we won't price-match routine maintenance. But can I chase a data center greenfield deal on my own, and what do I offer a customer who walks?" | A short "say yes / say no" list for deals |
| **Names the real challenge** | 7 | 6 | Commoditization of maintenance is named clearly. It doesn't say why Digital Services is only #4 today: price, adoption, or sales skills. | One line on the root cause of digital's low share |
| **Makes a hard bet** | 8 | 7 | The no on price is real and uncomfortable. But digital, data centers, and the core are all pursued, so the bet feels spread. | Say which one wins when they compete for people |
| **Cascadable** | 4 | 5 | "10M, 5M, and +10 satisfaction are global. What's my region's number?" No rule for what counts as leveraged growth. | Regional split of KR1 and KR2, plus the attribution rule |
| **Coherent** | 5 | 6 | Defend the core, refuse price fights, and enter data centers with flat headcount feels like three asks on the same team. | Show how digital makes the core cheaper to serve, freeing capacity |
| **Committed** | 3 | 3 | Nothing says what budget, people, or incentives move, or what we stop doing. | Name the investment, the incentive change, and one thing de-prioritized |

**Average: about 5.5 / 10.** Clear at the top, weaker as it cascades to regions. Biggest lift: a regional split of KR1 and KR2 with an owner for each, which raises both Cascadable and Committed.
