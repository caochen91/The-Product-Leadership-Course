# Scenario 2: Meridian (Enterprise Software)

## Context

Imagine you are a Product Leader at **Meridian**, a construction project management platform used on large commercial projects.

Meridian is highly valued by executives, project managers, finance teams, and compliance functions because it centralizes documentation, contract management, reporting, and project controls.

As the platform expanded, it became more powerful but also more complex.

Field teams, including superintendents and site foremen, increasingly avoid using the system. Instead, they communicate through personal messaging applications, spreadsheets, photos, and informal workflows.

Although Meridian is the official system of record, the people closest to the job site often operate outside the platform.

## The Challenge

A field adoption crisis is emerging.

Enterprise buyers value the depth and compliance capabilities of Meridian. However, field users find the system difficult and time-consuming to use.

Leadership is divided:

- Enterprise teams want deeper reporting and analytics.
- Product teams want a simplified mobile experience.
- Sales teams want additional enterprise features for large accounts.

The organization can only make one significant platform investment.

## Constraints

- Existing enterprise functionality cannot be removed.
- Compliance capabilities must remain intact.
- The platform cannot undergo a complete rebuild.
- Mobile-first competitors continue gaining traction.
- Success depends on winning frontline user adoption.

## Goal

Determine how Meridian can become indispensable to field personnel while preserving the enterprise-grade capabilities that large customers depend on.
