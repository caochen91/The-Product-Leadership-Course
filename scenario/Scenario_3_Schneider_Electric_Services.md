# Scenario 3: Schneider Electric Services Growth Strategy

## Context

Imagine you are a Business Development and Product Leader within the **Schneider Electric Services** division, responsible for helping accelerate growth across the service business.

For decades, Schneider's services organization has grown through a large installed base of electrical distribution equipment, critical power systems, building automation systems, and industrial automation solutions.

The service business is built on nine key pillars:

- **EcoConsult**: advisory and consulting services
- **EcoCare**: maintenance service contracts
- **EcoFit**: modernization and retrofit of the installed base
- **Digital Services**: remote monitoring, predictive maintenance, and connected asset management
- **Field Services**: on-site intervention, commissioning, and repair
- **Spare Parts**: spare parts and repairs business
- **Cybersecurity**: OT and infrastructure cybersecurity services
- **Sustainability**: decarbonization and sustainability services
- **Energy Advisory**: energy procurement, efficiency, and energy management advice

### Today's core pillars (ranked by current contribution)

1. EcoCare (maintenance contracts)
2. EcoFit (modernization)
3. Field Services
4. Digital Services
5. Spare Parts
6. EcoConsult
7. Cybersecurity

### Future growth potential (ranked)

1. Digital Services
2. EcoCare
3. Sustainability and Advisory
4. EcoFit
5. Cybersecurity

Historically, growth was closely tied to hardware installations.

However, the market is changing.

Customers increasingly view routine maintenance as a commodity and are willing to evaluate lower-cost third-party service providers. At the same time, customers face increasing pressure to reduce energy costs, improve operational uptime, extend asset life, and meet sustainability commitments.

Competitors are investing heavily in:

- Predictive maintenance
- Digital service platforms
- Remote operations
- AI-driven analytics
- Outcome-based service agreements

While Schneider remains a trusted technology leader, executives disagree on where the next phase of growth should come from.

## The Challenge

Three strategic schools of thought have emerged.

### Strategy A: Defend the Core

Strengthen maintenance offerings and increase service coverage to protect installed-base revenue.

### Strategy B: Scale Digital Services

Accelerate investment in remote monitoring, predictive analytics, and connected asset management platforms.

### Strategy C: Data Center, Energy Landscape and AI Transformation

Build services around the fastest-moving forces in the market: the data center build-out driven by AI workloads, a changing energy landscape (electrification, grid constraints, on-site generation and microgrids, energy price volatility), and AI transformation of how assets are operated and serviced. This means lifecycle service offers for data centers and critical power, energy advisory for customers facing new power constraints, and AI-enabled service delivery across the portfolio.

Limited resources mean only one strategic growth initiative can be prioritized.

## Constraints

- Core maintenance revenue cannot be disrupted.
- Headcount growth is limited.
- Customers expect measurable value within a short timeframe.
- Independent service providers compete aggressively on price.
- Investments must demonstrate business impact within three years.
- Existing customer contracts and commitments must be preserved.

## Goal

Define how Schneider Electric Services should grow over the next three years, identify the highest-priority strategic investment area, and determine how Schneider can differentiate beyond traditional maintenance services while creating sustainable recurring revenue growth.

## Study Framework

Use these four steps to work through the scenario.

### Step 1: Map the portfolio (today vs. future)

Place each pillar by its current rank and its future growth rank to see what role it plays.

| Pillar | Today rank | Future rank | Role |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| EcoCare | 1 | 2 | Core engine: protect and keep growing |
| EcoFit | 2 | 4 | Cash engine: monetize the installed base |
| Field Services | 3 | Not ranked | Enabler: delivery capacity for every other pillar |
| Digital Services | 4 | 1 | Growth engine: biggest gap between today and future |
| Spare Parts | 5 | Not ranked | Defend: margin and installed-base lock-in |
| EcoConsult | 6 | 3 (with Sustainability) | Emerging: entry point for advisory-led deals |
| Cybersecurity | 7 | 5 | Emerging bet: build capability, partner where possible |
| Sustainability / Energy Advisory | Not ranked | 3 | Emerging: rising with customer pressure on energy and carbon |

Questions to answer:
- Which pillars fund the growth (cash engines) and which consume investment (growth engines)?
- Where is the biggest gap between today's rank and future rank? That is where investment has the highest leverage.

### Step 2: Link each strategy to the pillars

| Strategy | Pillars it mainly strengthens | Pillars it puts at risk |
|--------|--------|--------|
| A: Defend the Core | EcoCare, Field Services, Spare Parts | Digital Services falls further behind competitors |
| B: Scale Digital Services | Digital Services, EcoCare (connected contracts), EcoFit (data-driven upgrades) | Field headcount and skills if the shift is too fast |
| C: Data Center, Energy Landscape and AI Transformation | Sustainability and Energy Advisory, EcoConsult, EcoFit, Cybersecurity | Focus and delivery capacity across many new offers |

### Step 3: Score each strategy against the constraints

Score each strategy 1 (weak) to 5 (strong) on each criterion, then compare totals.

| Criterion | A | B | C |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| Protects core maintenance revenue | | | |
| Works within limited headcount | | | |
| Shows measurable customer value quickly | | | |
| Defends against low-cost independent service providers | | | |
| Shows business impact within three years | | | |
| Preserves existing contracts and commitments | | | |
| Fits the future growth ranking | | | |
| Creates recurring, differentiated revenue | | | |

### Step 4: Build the recommendation

1. **Choose** one strategy as the priority and say why the other two lose.
2. **Sequence** it over three years: what happens in year 1, year 2, and year 3.
3. **Protect the core**: explain how EcoCare and Field Services stay safe while you invest.
4. **Measure**: pick 3 to 5 metrics (for example recurring revenue share, connected assets under contract, renewal rate, attach rate of digital to EcoCare, margin per contract).
5. **Name the risks** and how you would de-risk them (pilots, partners, pricing models).

Tip: a strong answer often combines strategies by sequence rather than choosing in isolation, for example using Digital Services to make EcoCare harder to commoditize, then applying it to data center and energy customers.
