# Scenario 1: Fable (Consumer Product)

## Context

Imagine you are a Product Leader at **Fable**, a mental wellness application serving adults aged 25-45. Fable grew rapidly through a simple and effective experience focused on guided meditation, sleep stories, and breathing exercises. The product now has millions of active users and is widely recognized as a trusted solution for managing stress and anxiety.

The product has historically succeeded by solving a very specific problem exceptionally well. Users often arrive during periods of acute stress, engage deeply for several weeks, experience measurable improvement, and then gradually stop using the application because they no longer feel they need it.

Meanwhile, competitors continue expanding their offering through AI-powered personalization, coaching experiences, habit formation programs, and social engagement features.

## The Challenge

The product's success has created its own retention problem.

Users achieve the desired outcome and leave.

Leadership disagrees on the next phase of growth:

- Clinical advisors advocate structured mental wellness programs.
- Growth teams advocate community and social features.
- Content teams advocate expanding lifestyle and self-improvement content.

The organization can only fund one major initiative.

## Constraints

- Fable cannot reposition as a clinical healthcare service.
- Meditation and sleep content remain the core product.
- The existing freemium business model must remain.
- Resources are limited to a single strategic investment.
- The primary challenge is retaining users after their initial crisis period.

## Goal

Define where Fable should play after helping users overcome acute stress and determine the strategy that creates long-term engagement without compromising the brand's core value proposition.
