# Understanding Schneider Electric Services

A guided set of questions for getting up to speed on the Schneider Electric Services business. Work through the steps in order. Each step builds on the one before, following the Playing to Win cascade: winning aspiration, where to play, how to win, capabilities, and enablers. It then tests that picture against the competition.

Use the pillars and rankings in [Scenario 3](scenario/Scenario_3_Schneider_Electric_Services.md) as your reference point throughout.

**How to use this guide**

- Write your answers under each question as you learn them.
- Note where each answer came from (a person, a document, a dashboard).
- Mark answers you are unsure of with **(?)** and come back to them.
- Re-read your answers after 30, 60, and 90 days in the role.

---

## Step 1: Winning Aspiration

*What does winning look like for Schneider Electric Services?*

1. How does Schneider Electric describe its overall purpose and ambition, and where do services fit in it?
2. What is the stated ambition for the services business (revenue, growth rate, share of group revenue, margin)?
3. Is the goal to be the largest service provider, the most trusted, the most digital, or the most sustainable? Which matters most to leadership?
4. What share of revenue should be recurring (contracts, subscriptions) versus transactional (repairs, spare parts, one-off projects)?
5. Who are we trying to win with: customers, partners, the installed base, or new segments?
6. How is winning measured today? Which KPIs does the services leadership team review every month?
7. How does the services ambition support the group's broader goals in electrification, digitization, and sustainability?
8. If the services business succeeds in three years, what will customers say about Schneider that they don't say today?

---

## Step 2: Where to Play

*Which customers, markets, offers, and channels do we choose, and which do we avoid?*

### Customers and segments

1. Which customer segments generate the most service revenue today (data centers, buildings, industry, infrastructure, utilities, healthcare)?
2. Which segments are growing fastest, and which are shrinking or commoditizing?
3. How important are data centers and AI-driven power demand to the future of the service business?
4. Do we serve end users directly, through partners and system integrators, or both? Which channel is growing?

### Geography

5. Which regions and countries carry the most service revenue and growth?
6. Where do we have the strongest field service coverage, and where are the gaps?

### Offers (the pillars)

7. For each pillar (EcoConsult, EcoCare, EcoFit, Digital Services, Field Services, Spare Parts, Cybersecurity, Sustainability, Energy Advisory), what is its revenue, growth, and margin?
8. Why do EcoCare, EcoFit, and Field Services rank highest today? What makes them strong?
9. Why are Digital Services ranked first for future growth? What has to be true for that to happen?
10. Which pillars are standalone businesses, and which mainly attach to others (for example, Digital Services attached to EcoCare)?

### Installed base

11. How large is the installed base, and what share is under a service contract today?
12. What share of the installed base is connected (sending data)?
13. How much of the installed base is aging or near end of life, creating EcoFit opportunity?
14. Do we service non-Schneider (multi-vendor) equipment? Should we?

### Where not to play

15. Which segments, offers, or regions have we chosen not to pursue, and why?

## Step 3: How to Win

*Why should a customer choose Schneider Services over the alternatives?*

1. What is our value proposition for each core pillar, in the customer's words?
2. Do we win mainly on expertise (OEM knowledge), on coverage (speed and reach), on technology (digital and data), or on outcomes (uptime, energy savings)?
3. How does being the original equipment manufacturer give us an advantage that third-party providers cannot copy?
4. How do we price services: time and materials, fixed contracts, subscriptions, or outcome-based agreements?
5. Which offers create lock-in and long-term relationships, and which can customers easily switch away from?
6. How do the pillars reinforce each other? For example, how does a Digital Services subscription lead to EcoCare renewals or EcoFit projects?
7. How does each strategy in Scenario 3 (Defend the Core, Scale Digital Services, Data Center, Energy Landscape and AI Transformation) change how we win?
8. Where are we winning deals today, and where are we losing them? What reasons do customers give?

---

## Step 4: Capabilities

*What must the services team be great at to deliver how we win?*

1. How large is the field service workforce, and how is it distributed by region and skill?
2. Which skills are scarce or hard to hire (for example, medium-voltage, critical power, OT cybersecurity, data analytics)?
3. How strong are our remote monitoring and service centers? What can they diagnose and fix remotely?
4. Which digital platforms and tools do we use for monitoring, analytics, and asset management? How mature are they?
5. How do we turn asset data into insights the customer will pay for?
6. How fast and reliable is our spare parts supply chain?
7. How strong are our consulting and advisory capabilities (EcoConsult, Sustainability, Energy Advisory)?
8. How good are we at selling services, not just delivering them? Do sales teams have service targets?
9. Which capabilities do we build ourselves, and which do we get through partners or acquisitions?

---

## Step 5: Enablers (Management Systems)

*What systems, structures, and measures support those capabilities?*

1. How is the services organization structured (by pillar, by region, by segment)? Who owns the profit and loss?
2. How are product, sales, and service teams aligned? Where are the handoffs and friction points?
3. Which incentives and targets drive behavior in sales and service teams? Do they favor recurring revenue?
4. Which systems (CRM, service management, installed base data) do teams rely on? How reliable is the installed base data?
5. How are new service offers created, tested, and launched? Who decides?
6. How do we capture customer feedback and service quality (NPS, renewal rate, first-time fix rate)?
7. How are training and certification managed for field engineers?
8. Which governance forums decide on investments and priorities across the pillars?

## Step 6: Strengths and Weaknesses vs. Competition

*How do we compare with the competitors that matter?*

### Know the competitors

1. Who are our main OEM competitors in services (for example ABB, Siemens, Eaton, Vertiv, Honeywell, Johnson Controls), and where does each focus?
2. Who are the main independent service providers and local players, and in which segments do they compete?
3. Which software and digital players compete for the monitoring and analytics part of the value chain?

## Step 7: Key Vulnerabilities

*Where are we exposed, and what could hurt us most?*

1. Which part of core maintenance revenue is most at risk from lower-cost independent providers?
2. What happens to EcoCare renewals if customers come to see maintenance as a commodity?
3. If a competitor or software player owns the customer's data and monitoring platform, what happens to our position?
4. How exposed are we to field workforce shortages and an aging technician base?
5. Which customer segments could we lose if we are slow on data centers, the energy transition, or AI?
6. How dependent is service growth on new hardware sales? What happens if hardware sales slow?
7. Are we exposed in cybersecurity, either as a gap in our offer or as a risk to connected assets?
8. Which of the constraints in Scenario 3 (headcount, short time to value, three-year impact, existing contracts) makes us most vulnerable?
9. Which vulnerabilities are we actively addressing, and which have no owner today?

## Step 8: Bring It Together

*Summarize what you learned on one page.*

1. Write the services winning aspiration in one sentence.
2. List the three most important choices about where to play.
3. Describe how we win in two or three sentences.
4. Name the three capabilities that matter most and how strong each one is today.
5. Name the top three strengths and the top three vulnerabilities against competitors.
6. Based on all of this, which Scenario 3 strategy would you prioritize, and why?
7. What are the five questions you still can't answer, and who will you ask?
